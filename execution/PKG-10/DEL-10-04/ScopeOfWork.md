# DEL-10-04 — Project production dependency DAG

## Purpose and Objective Traceability

The project has one production dependency graph. For every deliverable it shows what that deliverable needs, from whom, under what condition and by when, including what is awaited from outside the project. Anyone choosing what to work on next can read from it what is ready and what is held, and can tell the project's production relationships apart from the route an undertaking has chosen and from what is actually executing. This deliverable contributes that graph, the examination that shows it is complete and free of cycles, and the owner's acceptance of an identified version of it.

It is the SOW-211 contribution: one project dependency graph, established before the 30% position (OPERATING_METHOD V4-OPS-02). It serves OBJ-006 by making production relationships and the decisions about them recoverable from files, and OBJ-010 by grounding the order of work in the accepted allocation and each deliverable's own scope. The accepted decomposition assigns it to PKG-10 as a data-model deliverable of the App project dependency owner; the owner accepts the examined current graph.

## Deliverable Definition — Ontology

- **OUT-001** — The identified project dependency graph and its interface account: the node inventory, what an edge means and which way it points, the relationships admitted, those held and those excluded with the source of each, and the contributions awaited from outside the project with their owners and points of need. Scope: SOW-211. Objectives: OBJ-006 and OBJ-010.
- **OUT-002** — The closure, examination and acceptance evidence for that graph: its coverage and accounting, the independent review, the owner's decisions on its basis and on a version, the accepted version kept unaltered with its handoff, and the currency evidence its consumers need. Scope: SOW-211. Objectives: OBJ-006 and OBJ-010.

Terms the product implements:

- **Node inventory.** The deliverables of the accepted decomposition. The inventory is fixed by the decomposition, not by which deliverables have declared anything; a deliverable whose declarations are missing, unreadable or invalid stays a node.
- **Relationship.** A consumer needs a stated contribution from a supplier under a stated condition. An edge points from the consumer to the supplier. A relationship carries the supplier, the consumer, the contribution and its condition or maturity, the point of need, its source, whether a person declared it or it was drawn from a scope, and whether it is presently satisfied. A reference or a statement of ownership is a production relationship only where its source establishes the specific exchange or constraint.
- **Declaration.** A deliverable's own statement of what it needs, kept in its `deliverable.yaml`. A supplier may be a deliverable, a package, a document, a named party outside the project, or unknown.
- **Admitted, held, excluded.** Every relationship, declared or drawn from a scope, is counted exactly once. It is admitted as an edge of the graph; or held as a candidate that would close a cycle, kept non-gating with its open question; or excluded with a reason, such as being the mirror of another declaration, not being a production dependency, or pointing outside the inventory.
- **Coverage.** The graph aims to cover the relationships of every node. That aim is not a claim that the coverage exists; what is missing is shown.
- **Interface account.** The contributions awaited from outside the project and the open matters that bear on them, each with its owner, its standing, its consequence and its point of need.
- **Version and acceptance.** A version is one identified state of the graph. The owner decides its basis (the objective, edge meaning, selection rules, inventory and exemptions, coverage limits and the treatment of cycles) and then the version itself. An accepted version is kept so that its exact content can be recovered and is not altered afterwards.
- **Currency.** Set against the live declarations, an accepted version is unchanged; or changed only in evidence, such as satisfaction; or departed from, where a node, an admitted or held relationship or a cycle has changed; or not comparable, where a source cannot be read. A deliverable touched by a departure or an incomplete comparison is pending: the graph gives it no verdict of ready or blocked.

Boundary:

The owner decides the graph's basis, the acceptance of a version, any consequential ruling to cut or merge a cycle, and the acceptance or rejection of a departure. An agent may record such a decision as it was given, with its actor and how it was relayed. Examining, recording and deciding are three different acts.

The graph uses their records at the points where it needs them and replaces none of them. A file prepared here is no evidence of outside delivery, agreement or adoption. The owner judges whether the project continues past the 30% position. Production, examination, release, adoption and professional acts keep their own actors and evidence, and accepting the graph establishes none of them.

## Production and Verification Method — Praxeology

Relationships are declared where they are needed: each deliverable states its own upstream needs in its `deliverable.yaml`, and the graph is derived from those declarations by a read-only query that cites, for every relationship, the file and entry that states it. The reverse links from a supplier to its consumers are derived and never declared a second time; a producer states an outward obligation only to a recipient outside the project. Deriving the graph writes nothing, runs no check a declaration names, and marks nothing complete, ready or accepted.

The graph is prepared in this order: recover the accepted allocation and the tracking in force; fix the inventory and the objective; obtain usable declarations through their owners; examine coverage and closure; prepare the basis decision; resolve or hold coupled work; assemble the candidate and have it independently examined; prepare the version decision; keep only what the owner actually accepted as the accepted version; hand over with the evidence of currency. Evidence that is current and suitable is reused. The two decisions may be put to the owner together for a small undertaking, after the independent review.

- **VER-001** — Comparison with a named reference: the graph's basis and node inventory are compared with the accepted decomposition's deliverables and the tracking in force, including the nodes with missing declarations, the exemptions, the coverage limits, the objective and the edge direction.
- **VER-002** — Comparison with a named reference: each relationship is traced to the declaration or scope sentence that states it and compared field by field, checking the contribution, condition, standing and satisfaction, the marking of declared against drawn relationships, the single counting of a relationship stated from both ends, and the routing of a disagreement to both owners.
- **VER-003** — Comparison with a named reference: the interface account and the excluded relationships are compared with the decomposition's external-dependency and open-issue rows as they now stand, examining a pending SWBPIPE delivery, PEC as not awaited, Domains with its provider undecided, and the supplier and adoption rows, to confirm that acceptance neither waits for outside completion nor implies that a dependent input is met.
- **VER-004** — Executable check (observes the admitted graph and the accounting of declarations): tests the admitted graph for cycles and duplicate edges, compares the sum of admitted, held and excluded relationships with the count of relationships declared or drawn from a scope, and checks that each held relationship is non-gating and names a case; the reviewer of VER-005 reads the closure report and each resolved case for its later closure evidence.
- **VER-005** — Judgment by an independent reviewer that did not assemble the graph: compares the candidate with the confirmed basis, reruns the checks of VER-002 and VER-004, examines the edge meaning, exclusions, mirrors and held work, records blocking and non-blocking findings, and compares each package and decision subject with the candidate as checked and with any later change of basis.
- **VER-006** — Comparison with a named reference: the decision record is compared with the owner's own words as relayed, and the content identity it gives with those of the kept version, manifest, handoff and pointer; the negative cases examined are a proposal or check relabelled as acceptance, an invented actor or date, changed content published under an earlier decision, and an observation treated as accepted.
- **VER-007** — Executable check, with judgment by an independent reviewer: the accepted version is compared with the live declarations for changes of inventory, admitted and held relationships and cycles and for unreadable sources, and each comparison is classed; the reviewer checks the pending marks, the absence of a ready or blocked verdict while pending, the unchanged accepted version, and each successor or rejection against the owner's decision.
- **VER-008** — Judgment by an independent reviewer: reads the handoff and reliance statements against four cases, namely a valid scope whose artifact is absent, definition before the first accepted graph, a held unresolved cycle, and a graph accepted with dependencies pending, and confirms that each keeps its real next condition with no prerequisite on itself and no invented act of position, progress, release or reliance.
- **VER-009** — Judgment by an independent reviewer: inspects the graph, packages, decision records and handoff for an act of a supplier, a neighbour or the owner, or an amendment of another's declaration, shown as done by this deliverable, and checks that preparation, examination, decision and recording each name their actual actor.

## Governing Values and Decisions — Axiology

- **AX-001** — Reliance rests on the accepted allocation and on decisions the owner actually made. Better means fewer relationships that work relies on without a declaration that states them; never by treating a read manual, a passing check, a merge or a moved pointer as the owner's acceptance.
- **AX-002** — The dependency account is complete and truthful, in proportion to its readers: the graph owner, the independent examiner, the owner at the basis, version and 30% decisions, and managers selecting routes. Better means fewer nodes whose relationships are missing or unexamined; never by removing a relationship to obtain an acyclic graph, by amending another owner's declaration, or by keeping a second record of execution.
- **AX-003** — The graph orders work; it does not perform it. Better means less authorized work held for a dependency it does not have; never by showing an input as met, a hold as lifted or an outside contribution as delivered without that fact's own evidence.
