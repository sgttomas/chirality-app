# DEL-01-04 — Native requests, outcomes and attachments

## Purpose and Objective Traceability

This deliverable is where the person meets Codex in a conversation and where the person's own acts are captured. The person answers the requests Codex raises, sees how each turn actually ended, hands Codex files whose content is known, and follows a workflow draft until it is registered. When something needs an act that only a person may perform — marking work checked, approving, relying, registering a workflow, deciding a prepared matter — the App offers one control that only the person can operate and records exactly what was confirmed.

It is the UX_UI_SLICE `DEL-01-04` of PKG-01 in the accepted decomposition, serving OBJ-001 and OBJ-002.

| Accepted scope | Contribution | Source |
|---|---|---|
| SOW-005 | Native approvals and questions, answered through the execution owner's register | PRD V4-APP-01, V4-EXE-02 |
| SOW-014 | Requests and responses keep Codex's own meaning; plans, tools and delegation are `DEL-01-03`'s share | PRD V4-APP-04; ARCHITECTURE V4-ARC-05 |
| SOW-129 | Request, draft, turn-outcome and attachment interactions with the identity of supplied content, and the receiving side of the workflow workspace; the plan interaction is `DEL-01-03`'s share | ARCHITECTURE §3; PRD V4-WF-02 |

Other sources are PRD V4-APP-02 and V4-APP-03 (a new conversation has no model and offers the conversation roles), V4-EXE-01 to V4-EXE-04, V4-AUT-03 to V4-AUT-05 and V4-REC-05, and EXAMINATION V4-EXM-10 and V4-EXM-11. The person's keychain protection act it offers relies on the signed installed identity `DEL-01-06` supplies (release target, installed custody and keychain protection).

Terms:

- **Request card.** The presentation of one outstanding server request, drawn by the App from the host's request record and never from text an agent supplies. It names who asks, from the thread the request names: the agent of the conversation it belongs to, or a delegated agent by its role and name as Codex reports them, with what Codex has not reported said as not reported and an asker the App cannot place said as not established. A request the App does not offer an answer for (an App tool call, an external-token refresh, an unfamiliar method) is shown as an information line stating the error it was answered with and the App rule that answered.
- **Explicit decline.** The request's own negative form, or for a kind that has none, the native empty answer. A request Codex resolves itself is shown as resolved by Codex, never as an answer.
- **Agent's question.** Codex's own question request. When Codex offers the tool, the conversation's agent can raise it at any App permission level. Codex supports it in plan mode, and the App enables it for ordinary conversations (`DEL-01-05`, the account home term). It waits until the person answers or declines, or the turn is interrupted; Codex's mark that such a question need not block changes nothing the App does. A delegated agent cannot raise one: Codex refuses its call. A question still waiting when Codex's process ends is not raised again afterwards; it is shown as a past request and never as waiting. A question an agent writes in its message is not a request and is not counted as one.
- **Waiting on you.** One line seen on every view, giving the number of records waiting on the person and how many of them are requests from Codex, which opens to one entry per record: the person's answerable requests in the request register of the home in use, with a home not in use given as one entry per conversation with its count; checkpoint arrivals whose recorded disposition is waiting, in a run that has not ended; returns awaiting review in the return-review queue; and pending decision packages (`DEL-06-02`). It is a view over those records, never a second queue or a second source of status: each source shows what its latest reading positively observed, and the next reading replaces it: the list keeps no memory of entries between readings. An entry leaves when its source records the answer or when the source's latest reading no longer holds it, so an item seen in one limited reading can drop from view when the next reading does not hold it, and after a change of active home the requests of the home left are shown only as that home's overview reads them, or named as not read. What is retained is per kind of read, not per source: when the status read, the returns read or the decisions read fails, the list uses the last successful response to that kind of read, if any. Its waiting entries are shown apart, as last read at its time, uncounted and with their current state unknown. For the status response, its waiting entries and sources it had not read whole are grouped in one block; a response showing nothing waiting is described in the coverage lines. Its limitations remain shown, and each source it had not read whole is identified as not read or read with limits, as it was then. The retained reading keeps its home and generation, where applicable, and its read limits, and another home's reading is never shown in its place. Nothing is said of whether an earlier response held more. Each entry says what waits, who asks, who must respond, what it concerns and who integrates the result, each as its source records it or as not recorded, and leads to the record's own control; nothing in the list answers, dismisses or settles anything (PRD V4-PM-07). It gives a definite count, or says "nothing is waiting", only when every source's reader has said its reading is whole; otherwise it names each source not read whole, with its reason and the time of its read (for a run reopened after the App restarted whose checkpoints are not read: the time the project's records were last read for it, where the host gives that time, and otherwise the time the status response that carried it was requested, said as that and never as a time its checkpoints or its records were read), says of each source read with limits that its reading may omit waiting items, counts only what is observed now and says "status incomplete". Where a reading of a run reopened after the App restarted does not establish the latest recorded disposition of its checkpoint arrivals, it names that run as not read, with its reason; where it cannot establish whether an act already recorded answers an arrival, it says so, with its reason, and does not list the arrival as waiting. It is not fit to ship in 4.0.0 until the recovered display of AC-013 (g) has been observed on the candidate.
- **Navigator read.** One read, which writes, starts and sends nothing, of every project folder the records store's registry lists (`DEL-04-03`) and, under each, the conversations the App holds for it: those Codex has listed to the App, those loaded, and those the App's own records name. For the project open in the running App it also gives, for each conversation, the latest run and how many runs name it, taken from the App's last reading of that project's recorded runs (made at launch, at the person's Reopen and after a recorded run is ended) and from the runs this running App holds, and it says when that reading was made. It makes no reading of any project's records itself: the records of a listed project that is not open are not read, and the read says so for that project; it likewise states each list, registry or reading that was not made or that reported limits. A conversation is placed under the project the App recorded for it; else under the listed project whose recorded folder is the folder Codex reports for it; else among the conversations without a project; else under its reported folder, shown as having no identity. A reported folder places only a conversation whose project the App has not recorded and is never taken as the App's record of its project.
- **Waiting words.** The App's words that open each entry of "Waiting on you", one for each kind of record: "a question is waiting" for a question or input request from Codex; "a tool permission is waiting" for a tool-permission request; "questions or tool permissions are waiting in another home", with their count, for a home not in use; "a checkpoint was reached; your act is not yet recorded" for a checkpoint arrival that no act or decline on record answers; "a return is waiting for review"; and "a decision is waiting". "The agent was asked to pause" is said only of a point whose record shows that the agent was asked to pause, "a turn completed" only of a completed turn a source records as what waits, and "the call was not answered before Codex stopped; it will not be asked again" only of an App-supplied call a source records as left unanswered when Codex stopped; an arrival is never said to be any of them. No waiting word, and no sentence of the App's in the list, says that work is held or blocked.
- **Arrival card.** The entry of "Waiting on you" for a checkpoint arrival. It gives, in this order, its waiting words; "Asked at the workflow's checkpoint" with the checkpoint's name as recorded for the arrival, marked as text the App did not write, and, where the run records a request for the act, who requested it as recorded, marked the same way; who must respond, from the verified checkpoint declaration ("you" or "the accountable professional"), or "not recorded" where that actor declaration is absent or cannot be verified; what it concerns: the run and, where the run names one, its conversation; and that who integrates the result is not recorded. It sits under its run's conversation and ends with "Go to it", which opens the run's recorded evidence in the workflow studio, where the run panel shows the checkpoint as plan guidance, each arrival with its recorded disposition and whether a request for the act was observed, for a run reopened after the App restarted its act-record projection, shown apart from its recorded disposition, and where the act is performed. The card answers, dismisses and settles nothing and opens no act control.
- **Act-record projection.** For a run reopened after the App restarted, what the reading finds that an act or decline already in the project's act records, and not yet counted in the run's record, does to one of its arrivals, by the rule the arrival act control uses: none answers it; one answers it and is not yet counted; or whether one does is unknown, with the reason, as when a record on the arrival's file cannot be settled or the act records cannot be read. It is made for each arrival of a run not recorded as ended, bound to that run and that exact arrival, from one read of the act records for the reading. It creates and changes no act, run or capture record and none of the run's recorded entries, and is never a recorded disposition; reading the act records may create or refresh the App's derived query cache of them in its records store, as the arrival act control's reading does. The derived cache is created in the index folder of the store the App has bound. The path is checked before creation and the binding is checked again after opening; replacement of the store folder between those steps is not defended against, as on the live path.
- **Turn outcome.** Not started; in progress; in progress and waiting for the person's answer; completed; interrupted, with its cause (by the person, by quit, by Stop Codex, after a cancel answer, or cause not observed); failed, with Codex's message; or outcome unknown.
- **Supplied-content identity.** The content identity of a file taken when the person selects it and again immediately before the host attempts to write the turn. Each attachment has a supply record stating how it was carried (its bytes supplied in the turn, as an image input, or only its path named for the agent to read) and that provider adoption is not observed.
- **Draft transition.** A change of a workflow draft's standing reported by the workflow workspace. This deliverable shows transitions and never produces one.
- **App act control.** A standing control that presents one act kind on one piece of App content and, on the person's confirmation in a native surface the host owns, produces capture evidence and a direct-capture human-act record. An *offer* fixes the act kind, the subject with its content identity, scope, purpose, the actor requirement and the arrival it answers, if any; for A16 it also fixes the decision standing of this request and of other requests naming the same file path, as the decision view derives it. A native A16 capture binds the SHA-256 digest of the exact marked statement handed to the native confirmation; this does not establish that macOS displayed those bytes or that the person read them.
- **Act kinds served.** On App files and outputs: mark checked, approve (engineering approval) and rely (professional reliance), each with a decline. On workflow content: register workflow revision — one reviewed draft, one existing library entry reviewed in place, or several library entries in one act — and re-confirm a registered revision for use. On a decision package: decide, choosing one alternative the package names. Setting a grant and accepting a proposal are not offered, because the App establishes no such setting and holds no proposal on App content.
- **Capture seal.** A keyed digest over a capture's sealed facts; a valid digest shows consistency with possession of that key over the covered facts. With the file-key provider, any process running as the same user can read the key. With the keychain provider, verification shows consistency with possession of a key held in the person's keychain under the keychain's protection. This keychain protection is the source's intent and is not established on an installed build until release criterion 8. No seal establishes who wrote the capture, that a person was present, or that they judged.
- **Keychain protection.** The state after the person's act "Protect registered workflows with your keychain": the sealing key and the workflow approval policy are held in the macOS keychain under the signed App's identity. They are generic-password items in the data-protection keychain, of service `com.chirality.app.v4.capture-seal`, in the App's own access group, which equals its `com.apple.application-identifier` entitlement: the provisioning profile's `ApplicationIdentifierPrefix`, a dot and the bundle identifier; they are readable only while the device is unlocked, stay on this device and are not synchronised. For this the App's own code needs the entitlements `com.apple.application-identifier` and `com.apple.developer.team-identifier`, granted by a provisioning profile in the bundle, and no `keychain-access-groups` entitlement, because the App's own access group is its default one. Key rotation is unavailable in 4.0.0.
- **Identity not verified.** The person is recorded from what the App can observe — the name set in the App, the operating-system account and the Codex account when Codex reports one — and every record says the identity is not verified.

## Completion and Reliance Basis — Epistemology

- **REQ-001** — Cards keep Codex's approval and question semantics and send Codex's own content unchanged. Where a request lists the answer forms it accepts, exactly those are offered. Grant, deny, answer and explicit decline go through the register, and a refused answer leaves the card waiting with the reason shown. Silence, elapsed time and closing the window are never an answer; no App rule declines a waiting request after any period. A tool-permission card says it is tool permission only and never uses the words accept, approve or approval as its own. A tool-permission card says, in the person's terms, what each answer it offers lets Codex do: that an allowed command runs once outside Codex's sandbox, or with more access than it gives where the conversation's profile is not one of Codex's own; that a rule lets every later matching command in the home do so with no request to the person, and where the rule is kept; how many files a change touches and where each is, marking any outside the project folder, from the paths Codex gave; and, where Codex offers no way to refuse and continue, that refusing interrupts the turn. Each card names who asks, and so does each group of waiting requests. Text an agent supplies inside a card (a command, a path, a reason, a question's wording, an option's label, a server name, a rule's words) is marked as the agent's in what is seen and in what the accessibility layer reports, kept apart from the App's words and from the controls, with its line breaks and control characters made visible and never acted on; an answer's control carries the App's words, never the agent's (PRD V4-PM-07). An answer to a question is conversation input, never act evidence. The App shows how many requests wait for the person, also when no conversation window is open, as part of "Waiting on you", the one list of what waits on the person.
- **REQ-002** — Turn and outcome display states only observed events; an unavailable outcome stays unknown. Rendered state, Codex's state, a settlement, a reply write and a received acknowledgment stay distinguishable, and a completed primary turn says nothing about descendants. A reopened or reloaded view shows the recovered state and outstanding requests it is given. Interrupting a turn, ending a run and stopping Codex are shown as distinct, and an interrupted turn is never shown as an ended run. The App offers Stop Codex and Restart Codex and presents the question before them and before the App's own Quit when work is live (DEL-01-02). A new conversation shows that no model is selected until the person chooses; the last explicit choice for the project may be offered and is never applied without the person's choice. A run that cannot start for want of a model reads "run not started — no model selected", and an ordinary conversation "not started — no model selected". A new conversation offers the conversation roles with the default preselected and clearable and no role allowed; the role is fixed for the conversation, and another role opens a new conversation with a handoff summary the person can edit. An agent's proposal of the next workflow, or its report that a workflow finished, is offered for the person to confirm and starts or ends nothing by itself. Turns are composed here, with the collaboration mode from `DEL-01-03` and the run-start text, the run-end line and a trial text from `DEL-02-02` placed unchanged. A control or heading here is named by the App's words alone, except a conversation choice's title, as follows. Where the person chooses among conversations, other than among the navigator's entries, each choice shows the App's words and an identifier that tells it apart from every other choice shown in that list (two entries that carry the same identity are told apart by their entry number), then the conversation's title as Codex supplies it, set apart from the App's words, with its line breaks and control characters made visible and a long title shortened with how much of it is shown said. The list says once, in the App's words, that its titles are supplied by Codex; each choice's accessible name gives the App's words, then who supplied the title, then the title. Only that name and its control respond to a click. The conversation's model and provider stand beside it, marked as text the App did not write, and may also reach the control as its accessible description, never its name, after the App's words saying who supplied them and with their line breaks and control characters made visible. In the navigator an entry's visible label is the conversation's title as Codex supplies it and nothing else that Codex supplies: on one line in the navigator's type, shortened with an ellipsis, with its line breaks and control characters made visible. An entry whose conversation has no title reads "Untitled chat" with its time. Each entry shows its time; entries with the same title are told apart by their times and, where the times are equal, by their entry numbers in the accessible description. That the title is supplied by Codex is said once in each entry's accessible description, and in the conversation's tools, where the identifier and the whole title are shown; the navigator's list does not carry that sentence and shows no model or provider. The App's own state words on an entry (Your move, Needs answer, Approval needed, Agent says finished, and the mark of a running turn) are shown only in the App's own mark on the entry, in a form a title cannot take and never in the title's type, and in the entry's accessible name they come before the title, so that a title imitating them is still read as a title. This exception names the navigator's entries only; every other place where the person chooses among conversations keeps the form above. A conversation's relationship to another is shown from the relationship recorded, never from its title.
- **REQ-003** — The identity of supplied content is taken at selection and again immediately before the host attempts to write the turn; if it changed, or the file is missing or no longer a file, the send is held, nothing is sent, and the file is not sent until the person confirms the current content. What is sent is the content as selected; a change made after that last check is not detected. Same-named items with different content are different attachments. A selection or a send is never reported as provider adoption, review, registration or an outcome.
- **REQ-004** — The draft view shows a new or changed workflow as a draft until an actual reviewed registration is received, keeps its source-qualified identity and reviewed content identity, and shows a collision or refusal as the workspace reports it, with no silent overwrite or rebind. A transition the workspace did not report, or a registration without its act record, is shown as an error of the feed.
- **REQ-005** — Execution, proposal acceptance, checking, approval and professional reliance are shown as separate acts with their own actor, subject and evidence; none implies another, and no order among independently evidenced acts is imposed. A proposal decision reads "accept". An act actually performed is shown with the person as actor and the recorder distinct; where its bound content changed, the lapse is shown. At a workflow checkpoint the agent asks the person for the act, and nothing here pauses, interrupts, refuses or captures an act because an arrival was recorded; an earlier act of the required kind on still-current content is shown as counting, with its time, and several acts may answer one arrival together, each shown with what it covers.
- **REQ-006** — This deliverable performs no act that CLM-002, CLM-003 and CLM-004 give elsewhere.
- **REQ-007** — The requirements above hold whether or not v3 interface code is reused.
- **REQ-008** — The App act control can be operated only by the person: no agent tool, MCP operation, App command reachable from a conversation, App rule, supplier request or interface script operates it or produces its record. The offer is composed by the host from authoritative sources; text an agent wrote is never the act kind, subject, scope or purpose. Capture follows only the person's confirmation in a native surface; in the confirmation for registering, re-confirming or deciding, Return does not act. The confirmation is accepted only for the offer shown and only while the subject still has the content identity the offer fixed. It serves one act kind, one subject and one confirmation at a time, offers the decline only where a decline exists for the kind, is available whether or not an arrival was recorded, and answers no pending supplier request. Each capture yields capture evidence before any record is written and a direct-capture record in the evidence-record format. No operating-system presence check is made per act, and the control states its limits where the person can read them.
- **REQ-009** — Every capture is sealed with this installation's key, and a capture without a valid seal is reported as not verifiable. Before keychain protection is set up the key is in the App's data folder, and the App says that a process able to write there could forge a capture. "Protect registered workflows with your keychain" is the person's native act: it enrols the key in the keychain, after which the workflow approval policy is stored there as "Require verified approval" and a sealed registration is admitted after a cold start. It is available only on a signed build whose installed integrity holds; elsewhere it is shown unavailable with the reason. Lowering the workflow approval policy to "Allow disk records" takes the person's native confirmation bound to the current setting; no file, message from the web view or environment value lowers it, and a keychain failure never lowers it.
- **REQ-010** — The App places the run's state, the checkpoint overlay, the standing display and the control that ends a run, as `DEL-02-03` and `DEL-04-02` define them, with their meanings unchanged.
- **REQ-011** — The permission level (PRD V4-AUT-06) is a setting the person can change at any time, not a step before use. Until the person chooses one, the App's default level applies, which is full access, and no conversation is held for a choice outside the exception below. The person chooses a level, for the default for new conversations or for one existing conversation, through the App's level control; no agent tool, MCP operation, App command reachable from a conversation, App rule or supplier request makes or changes that choice. A choice is recorded in the App ledger as the person's; the App's default is never recorded as a choice. The control before a conversation starts says "Changing the level here changes the default for new conversations and for saved conversations that have no level of their own. Conversations with their own level keep it." An existing conversation whose host-reported chosen scope is default says "This conversation follows the default. Changing the default changes its level until you set a level for this conversation." A next-conversation-only scope is refused, never reinterpreted as the default. A created or forked conversation keeps its inherited level and origin through a separate recorded association, so a later change of the default for new conversations does not change that conversation, also after relaunch. If saving an inherited association is not confirmed, the conversation keeps its level for the session and the App says "The App could not confirm that this conversation's level was saved. If it is not saved before the App closes, reopening follows the default for new conversations then in force, which could allow more than this level, unless you set its level." That failure retains the existing persistence-error standing and creates no new ask; it does not apply to a failed explicit choice write. A session-only level and its inherited associations are never saved and are dropped on a later complete read. The session-only non-persistence promise applies only where no usable store could be asked to save that choice. After an append with an unknown outcome, the level in force and whether saving was confirmed are separate: saving is not confirmed, never not recorded. If the fallback applied the level, the App says "The App could not confirm the level was saved. It is in force for this session." If it did not apply the level, the App says "The App could not confirm the level was saved. This session's level was not changed; reopening will show the level the records then hold." Neither case implies that the records were unchanged or that the choice disappears at quit. As the one exception, the person is asked to choose before a conversation runs only where the App cannot show that applying its default would not raise a level the person had set: a recorded choice that no longer matches the App's level table requires a choice again whether or not the account home holds earlier Codex state. With no usable recorded choice, the App also asks when its whole ledger holds no entry from an earlier session but the account home holds state from an earlier Codex start, when the records cannot be read and that state is present, or when the home-state probe is unknown. The App's default is preselected so one press continues, and the App says why. Established absence of earlier Codex state permits the first-run default when the records cannot be read, with the unread-records disclosure and the saving outcome shown for any later choice. A failed fresh read preserves an already known narrower level. A whole validated reading with earlier session entries and no choice establishes only that no choice was found; the default applies. Removal or replacement of individual ledger entries by writes outside the App is not detected and can expose an older choice or the default (LIM-022). This limitation does not exclude an App choice-write failure or corruption the store detects: neither is treated as a successful recorded choice, and a failed read follows the exception above.
- **REQ-012** — A checkpoint arrival is listed in "Waiting on you", as its arrival card, while the latest reading establishes the latest recorded disposition of that exact arrival as waiting in a run that has not ended and, for a run reopened after the App restarted, its act-record projection finds no act or decline on record that answers it. Positively supplying a waiting disposition means establishing that it is the latest disposition of that arrival: an older waiting entry in an incomplete disposition history is not enough. An unrelated limitation, such as missing actor attribution, does not hide an arrival whose latest waiting disposition is established. A limited reading shows those established entries with its limits and incomplete count; it establishes neither a definite total nor "nothing is waiting". Where the latest disposition cannot be established, the App says it is not known and does not list an older waiting entry as currently waiting. An arrival whose act-record projection finds an answering act or decline on record, not yet counted in the run's record, is not listed, and nothing is asked of the person for it; the run's evidence says so beside its recorded disposition, which stays as recorded and is never said to be "act not yet recorded". Where the projection is unknown, including when the project's act records cannot be read at all, the arrival is listed neither as waiting nor as answered, its run's reading is read with limits, and the reason is said. The positive-observation, failed-read retention, last-read time and count rules of the term "Waiting on you" remain in force. An arrival whose latest recorded disposition is anything else, an arrival recorded as performed when it was reached because an earlier act on still-current content counts, and every arrival of an ended run are not listed, and a later run lists none of an earlier run's arrivals. Where work waits, the App says what waits in the waiting words and never that work is held or blocked. Nothing here pauses, interrupts or holds a run because of an arrival, and where the run panel shows a declared checkpoint the App says that a pause there is the agent's cooperation and not a hold by the App (PRD V4-WF-05). After a relaunch, recovered checkpoint history is shown under these same rules; an omitted or unavailable history is named as not read with its reason, never treated as nothing waiting.

## Governing Values and Decisions — Axiology

- **AX-001** — Codex's own request and outcome meaning, truthfully shown. Better means fewer outcomes and answers shown as unknown where they were in fact observed, and never by showing an answer, an acknowledgment, a completion or an adoption that was not observed.
- **AX-002** — An act is the person's or it is nothing. Better means fewer routes by which anything other than the person's confirmation could produce an act record, and a larger share of captures a later reader can verify, and never by claiming a verified identity, by asking for an act where none is reserved, or by letting a failure lower a protection the person set.
- **AX-003** — What was supplied is what is said to have been supplied. Better means more attachments whose supplied bytes are known exactly, and never by naming content the App did not read or claiming the provider adopted it.
- **TBD-005** — Where shared contracts and interface components live is undecided (App and shared-contract owners). No common draft or attachment component is allocated here.
