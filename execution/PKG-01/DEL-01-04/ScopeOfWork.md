# DEL-01-04 — Native requests, outcomes and attachments

## Purpose and Objective Traceability

This deliverable is where the person meets Codex in a conversation and where the person's own acts are captured. The person answers the requests Codex raises, sees how each turn actually ended, hands Codex files whose content is known, and follows a workflow draft until it is registered. When something needs an act that only a person may perform — marking work checked, approving, relying, registering a workflow, deciding a prepared matter — the App offers one control that only the person can operate and records exactly what was confirmed.

It is the UX_UI_SLICE `DEL-01-04` of PKG-01 in the accepted decomposition, serving OBJ-001 and OBJ-002.

| Accepted scope | Contribution | Source |
|---|---|---|
| SOW-005 | Native approvals and questions, answered through the execution owner's register | PRD V4-APP-01, V4-EXE-02 |
| SOW-014 | Requests and responses keep Codex's own meaning; plans, tools and delegation are `DEL-01-03`'s share | PRD V4-APP-04; ARCHITECTURE V4-ARC-05 |
| SOW-129 | Request, draft, turn-outcome and attachment interactions with the identity of supplied content, and the receiving side of the workflow workspace; the plan interaction is `DEL-01-03`'s share | ARCHITECTURE §3; PRD V4-WF-02 |

Other sources are PRD V4-APP-02 and V4-APP-03 (a new conversation has no model and offers the conversation roles), V4-EXE-01 to V4-EXE-04, V4-AUT-03 to V4-AUT-05 and V4-REC-05, and EXAMINATION V4-EXM-10 and V4-EXM-11. It contributes to publication punchlist P1 (using an existing workflow), to the registration act of P2, to P3 (request and approval, cancellation, close, reopen and Quit), and it offers the person's keychain protection act of P4, which relies on the signed installed identity `DEL-01-06` supplies.

Terms:

- **Request card.** The presentation of one outstanding server request. A request the App does not offer an answer for (an App tool call, an external-token refresh, an unfamiliar method) is shown as an information line stating the error it was answered with and the App rule that answered.
- **Explicit decline.** The request's own negative form, or for a kind that has none, the native empty answer. A request Codex resolves itself is shown as resolved by Codex, never as an answer.
- **Turn outcome.** Not started; in progress; in progress and waiting for the person's answer; completed; interrupted, with its cause (by the person, by quit, by Stop Codex, after a cancel answer, or cause not observed); failed, with Codex's message; or outcome unknown.
- **Supplied-content identity.** The content identity of a file taken when the person selects it and again when the host writes the turn. Each attachment has a supply record stating how it was carried (its bytes supplied in the turn, as an image input, or only its path named for the agent to read) and that provider adoption is not observed.
- **Draft transition.** A change of a workflow draft's standing reported by the workflow workspace. This deliverable shows transitions and never produces one.
- **App act control.** A standing control that presents one act kind on one piece of App content and, on the person's confirmation in a native surface the host owns, produces capture evidence and a direct-capture human-act record. An *offer* fixes the act kind, the subject with its content identity, scope, purpose, the actor requirement and the arrival it answers, if any.
- **Act kinds served.** On App files and outputs: mark checked, approve (engineering approval) and rely (professional reliance), each with a decline. On workflow content: register workflow revision — one reviewed draft, one existing library entry reviewed in place, or several library entries in one act — and re-confirm a registered revision for use. On a decision package: decide, choosing one alternative the package names. Setting a grant and accepting a proposal are not offered, because the App establishes no such setting and holds no proposal on App content.
- **Capture seal.** A keyed digest over a capture, made with a key this installation holds, by which a reader tells a capture this App wrote from bytes another process wrote.
- **Keychain protection.** The state after the person's act "Protect registered workflows with your keychain": the sealing key and the workflow approval policy are held in the macOS keychain under the signed App's identity. Key rotation is unavailable in 4.0.0.
- **Identity not verified.** The person is recorded from what the App can observe — the name set in the App, the operating-system account and the Codex account when Codex reports one — and every record says the identity is not verified.

Boundary:

- **CLM-001** — The Rust host owns the register, composes offers, captures acts and writes records; the web view presents and sends only what the person initiates. A card holds no answer as authoritative, and closing or reloading a view loses no request and answers none.
- **CLM-002** — `DEL-01-01` supplies the register operations, native frames, refusal reasons and the installed-integrity result. `DEL-01-02` owns custody, the stop definitions, recovered state and the interrupt, quit and stop operations this deliverable presents. `DEL-01-03` owns plan, tool and delegation views and offers the plan-mode element and item anchors. `DEL-01-05` owns account, provider and model selection and reports the selection state and the Codex account.
- **CLM-003** — `DEL-02-02` owns the workflow workspace, review, registration, the workflow approval policy and the reviewed content a registration names; the person's registration act is captured here, and the registration that follows is the workspace's operation, reported beside the act and never as part of it. `DEL-02-03` owns runs, run ends, the checkpoint record and what its entries mean; `DEL-04-02` defines the checkpoint overlay and standing components that show them (CLM-004), under the disposition names of `DEL-02-01`; this deliverable places those components and owns how they behave in the App. `DEL-02-04` owns the role list and role guidance. `DEL-06-02` owns decision packages and their view.
- **CLM-004** — `DEL-04-01` owns act names, wording and the operation policy; `DEL-04-02` the checkpoint overlay and standing components; `DEL-04-03` the record format, writer and lapse standing. The person performs their own acts, and the accountable professional makes any professional judgment. A recorder is never the actor.
- **CLM-005** — The interactions here are required whatever code implements them; v3 interface components are optional sources that qualify only by meeting these criteria.

## Completion and Reliance Basis — Epistemology

- **REQ-001** — Cards keep Codex's approval and question semantics and send Codex's own content unchanged. Where a request lists the answer forms it accepts, exactly those are offered. Grant, deny, answer and explicit decline go through the register, and a refused answer leaves the card waiting with the reason shown. Silence, elapsed time and closing the window are never an answer; no App rule declines a waiting request after any period. A tool-permission card says it is tool permission only and never uses the words accept, approve or approval as its own. An answer to a question is conversation input, never act evidence. The App shows how many requests wait for the person, also when no conversation window is open.
- **REQ-002** — Turn and outcome display states only observed events; an unavailable outcome stays unknown. Rendered state, Codex's state, a settlement, a reply write and a received acknowledgment stay distinguishable, and a completed primary turn says nothing about descendants. A reopened or reloaded view shows the recovered state and outstanding requests it is given. Interrupting a turn, ending a run and stopping Codex are shown as distinct, and an interrupted turn is never shown as an ended run. The App offers Stop Codex and Restart Codex and presents the question before them and before a quit when work is live. A new conversation shows that no model is selected until the person chooses; the last explicit choice for the project may be offered and is never applied without the person's choice. A run that cannot start for want of a model reads "run not started — no model selected", and an ordinary conversation "not started — no model selected". A new conversation offers the conversation roles with the default preselected and clearable and no role allowed; the role is fixed for the conversation, and another role opens a new conversation with a handoff summary the person can edit. An agent's proposal of the next workflow, or its report that a workflow finished, is offered for the person to confirm and starts or ends nothing by itself. Turns are composed here, with the collaboration mode from `DEL-01-03` and the run-start text, the run-end line and a trial text from `DEL-02-02` placed unchanged.
- **REQ-003** — The identity of supplied content is taken at selection and again when the turn is written; if it changed, or the file is missing, the file is not sent until the person confirms the current content. Same-named items with different content are different attachments. A selection or a send is never reported as provider adoption, review, registration or an outcome.
- **REQ-004** — The draft view shows a new or changed workflow as a draft until an actual reviewed registration is received, keeps its source-qualified identity and reviewed content identity, and shows a collision or refusal as the workspace reports it, with no silent overwrite or rebind. A transition the workspace did not report, or a registration without its act record, is shown as an error of the feed.
- **REQ-005** — Execution, proposal acceptance, checking, approval and professional reliance are shown as separate acts with their own actor, subject and evidence; none implies another, and no order among independently evidenced acts is imposed. A proposal decision reads "accept". An act actually performed is shown with the person as actor and the recorder distinct; where its bound content changed, the lapse is shown. At a workflow checkpoint the agent asks the person for the act, and nothing here is raised, opened or notified because an arrival was recorded; an earlier act of the required kind on still-current content is shown as counting, with its time, and several acts may answer one arrival together, each shown with what it covers.
- **REQ-006** — This deliverable performs no act that CLM-002, CLM-003 and CLM-004 give elsewhere.
- **REQ-007** — The requirements above hold whether or not v3 interface code is reused.
- **REQ-008** — The App act control can be operated only by the person: no agent tool, MCP operation, App command reachable from a conversation, App rule, supplier request or interface script operates it or produces its record. The offer is composed by the host from authoritative sources; text an agent wrote is never the act kind, subject, scope or purpose. Capture follows only the person's confirmation in a native surface; in the confirmation for registering, re-confirming or deciding, Return does not act. The confirmation is accepted only for the offer shown and only while the subject still has the content identity the offer fixed. It serves one act kind, one subject and one confirmation at a time, offers the decline only where a decline exists for the kind, is available whether or not an arrival was recorded, and answers no pending supplier request. Each capture yields capture evidence before any record is written and a direct-capture record in the evidence-record format. No operating-system presence check is made per act, and the control states its limits where the person can read them.
- **REQ-009** — Every capture is sealed with this installation's key, and a capture without a valid seal is reported as not verifiable. Before keychain protection is set up the key is in the App's data folder, and the App says that a process able to write there could forge a capture. "Protect registered workflows with your keychain" is the person's native act: it enrols the key in the keychain, after which the workflow approval policy is stored there as "Require verified approval" and a sealed registration is admitted after a cold start. It is available only on a signed build whose installed integrity holds; elsewhere it is shown unavailable with the reason. Lowering the workflow approval policy to "Allow disk records" takes the person's native confirmation bound to the current setting; no file, message from the web view or environment value lowers it, and a keychain failure never lowers it.
- **REQ-010** — The App places the run's state, the checkpoint overlay, the standing display and the control that ends a run, as `DEL-02-03` and `DEL-04-02` define them, with their meanings unchanged.

## Production and Verification Method — Praxeology

Cards and views are built from what the register and the recovered state report and are disposable; nothing is retried by itself. Fixtures use invented material and exchanges recorded from the stock supplier. A check on a model of the control or with a stand-in confirmation does not stand in for the person's confirmation on the candidate.

- **VER-001** — Comparison with the replies Codex actually received: exercise each request kind with grant, denial, answer and decline, an answer the request does not offer, an unoffered request kind, silence and a closed window; read the wording of a tool-permission card and the waiting count with every conversation window closed.
- **VER-002** — Comparison with Codex's reported turn status and the recovered state supplied: drive each outcome state, a missing acknowledgment, close, reload and reopen, a descendant active after the primary turn, an interrupt, a run end and a Codex stop, Stop Codex, Restart Codex and Quit with and without live work, a new conversation with and without a last choice, the role offer and "continue as", and an agent's proposal and finish report.
- **VER-003** — Comparison with an independent digest of the files on disk and with what Codex's history returns for the turn: follow distinguishable files through selection, sending and display, change one between selection and sending, and use two same-named files.
- **VER-004** — Comparison with the workspace's own registration record: exercise draft, changed draft, reviewed registration, collision and refusal, a transition the workspace did not report, and a registration reported without its act record.
- **VER-005** — Comparison with acts an examiner watched: present separately evidenced acts, an act followed by a content change, an earlier act on current content, two acts that together answer one arrival, and the negative cases of execution success alone, agent text claiming approval and a recorded arrival.
- **VER-006** — Judgment by an independent reviewer: trace each act of REQ-006 to its owner's code and read what the candidate's results rest on.
- **VER-007** — Judgment by an independent reviewer: read the fixture set and its results against AC-001 through AC-005.
- **VER-008** — Comparison with acts an examiner watched, on the native App: the person confirms an act on an App file, a registration of one reviewed draft, of one entry in place and of two entries in one act, and a decision on a decision package, and declines where a decline exists; automation is attempted by each route of REQ-008; Return is pressed in a registration and a decision confirmation; content is changed after an offer; the offered declines and the stated limits are read; a question is left waiting while the person acts. Records are validated with the evidence-record owner's validator.
- **VER-009** — Comparison with the keychain's contents as the operating system reports them, on the installed signed candidate: the person performs the protection act, the App is quit and started cold, a sealed registered workflow is selected, a capture's seal is altered, the policy is lowered with and without the person's confirmation, and the keychain is made to fail; the notice shown before protection is read; the same is attempted on an unsigned build.
- **VER-010** — Comparison with `DEL-04-02`'s overlay and standing definitions and with the run's state and records as `DEL-02-03` keeps them: drive a run through start, a checkpoint arrival, an act and its end, and compare what is shown at each point with those definitions and records.
- **VER-011** — Comparison with Codex's own history of the turn: send a run-start turn, a run-end turn, a trial turn and a turn after plan mode, and compare the texts and the collaboration mode Codex holds with what `DEL-02-02` supplied and `DEL-01-03` offered.

## Governing Values and Decisions — Axiology

- **AX-001** — Codex's own request and outcome meaning, truthfully shown. Better means fewer outcomes and answers shown as unknown where they were in fact observed, and never by showing an answer, an acknowledgment, a completion or an adoption that was not observed.
- **AX-002** — An act is the person's or it is nothing. Better means fewer routes by which anything other than the person's confirmation could produce an act record, and a larger share of captures a later reader can verify, and never by claiming a verified identity, by asking for an act where none is reserved, or by letting a failure lower a protection the person set.
- **AX-003** — What was supplied is what is said to have been supplied. Better means more attachments whose supplied bytes are known exactly, and never by naming content the App did not read or claiming the provider adopted it.
- **TBD-005** — Where shared contracts and interface components live is undecided (App and shared-contract owners). No common draft or attachment component is allocated here.
