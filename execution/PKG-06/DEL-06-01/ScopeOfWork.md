# Bounded delegation and current work-graph records

## Purpose and Objective Traceability

This deliverable makes delegated work recoverable from files. It contributes the App's record of coordination as ordinary files in the person's project: what was asked of whom (the brief), what work is selected and how it depends (the undertaking's current work graph), and what was observed, returned, reviewed and integrated (the coordination log). It also contributes the App's reading of those files and its recording of what it observes of Codex's native delegation. A reader holding only the files can tell who owns each piece of selected work, what it waits for, what came back and what comes next, and is never told that a child ran, a return arrived, a review happened or a person decided when no record of that fact exists.

It is the DATA_MODEL_CHANGE `DEL-06-01` of PKG-06 in the accepted decomposition, owned by the App fleet-record owner.

## Deliverable Definition — Ontology

- **OUT-001** — The record definitions: the versioned formats of the brief, the work-graph revision and the coordination-log entries, published as schemas that a writer or a reader validates against. Supports SOW-083, SOW-084; OBJ-006.
- **OUT-002** — The App's reader and writer: reading a project's fleet records as one consistent snapshot with every unreadable part reported, and recording the App's own observations of native delegation and of related conversations. Supports SOW-083, SOW-084; OBJ-006.
- **OUT-003** — Record fixtures for basis, owner, return and continuation, including absent execution evidence, an interruption and a changed basis, with their results on an identified candidate. Supports SOW-083, SOW-084; OBJ-006.
- **OUT-004** — The compatibility account: each format version with its writers, its consumers and the rule a change follows. Supports SOW-185; OBJ-006, OBJ-007.

Terms:

- **Record.** Every fleet record carries its format and version, a record identity, its kind, its recorder and when it was written. A brief, a graph revision, a selector and a log entry about a work item also carry the undertaking they belong to. An App observation of a related conversation carries none. The recorder is an agent, a person, the App or an external party, with its role; a person's identity is marked as verified or not verified. A record is never edited: a correction is a new record that names the one it corrects and gives a reason, and both stay readable.
- **Brief.** One file for one delegated work item. It states the purpose; the basis, as references with content identity where known; the context supplied; the authority (what the delegate may decide, when it must escalate, and what is reserved to the person); the tools; the write scope; and the expected return, with its form and return path. It names who prepared it and the role it is for. A changed brief is a new brief that supersedes the earlier one.
- **Limit standing.** Each tool and write-scope limit in a brief, and the delegating role's own limit on a dispatch, says how it is enforced: *stated, not enforced*; *enforced by the supplier* or *enforced by the host*, each naming the mechanism that enforces it; or *unknown*. A stated limit grants nothing and prevents nothing by itself.
- **Work graph.** A numbered revision file for one undertaking. Each work item has an outcome, an owner, its brief, its write scope, its needs, whether it is selected, its results and what comes next. The graph holds selected work only and has no place for an executing state. It cites the project dependency graph and does not restate it.
- **Need.** A prerequisite of a work item, of one of four kinds, each satisfied only as follows. An *item* need: the needed item's recorded integration, or an external result. A *decision* need: a recorded act of the person, of the kind the decision package names, that cites the package's request and, for a decide act, chooses an alternative the package names. An *input* need: the named file inside the fleet folder. A *connector* need, which names its connector: a connector record whose declared standing supports reliance. A need is satisfied, outstanding or unknown; it is unknown whenever the records that would settle it cannot be read. A file's mere presence satisfies no decision or connector need.
- **Current graph.** A log entry, the selector, names one revision by record identity and by the content identity of its file. The latest selector holds. If the revision it names is missing, duplicated or no longer has the selected content, no graph is current and no earlier selector is used in its place. Earlier revisions and selectors stay readable.
- **Coordination log.** One append-only file for each project, one entry to a line, written by whoever is the actor or the recorder of each fact:

  | Entry | Recorded by | Establishes | Never establishes |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | dispatch observed | the App | a native Codex child and its parent, with its association | that the child did the work, returned or was reviewed |
  | child observed | the App | the child's status as Codex last reported it, unchanged | a return |
  | observation ended | the App | that observation of a child ended, with the cause (Codex stopped, Codex exited, App quit, App relaunch, or not observed) and the status its recorder had last read when it recorded the end | any outcome |
  | return recorded | whoever received it | the returned items with content identity, who returned them and the evidence | review or integration |
  | review recorded | the reviewer or the manager | the reviewer, the content reviewed, the findings and the verdict (no blocking findings; findings to repair; not concluded) | checking, acceptance or approval by a person |
  | integration recorded | the integrator | the return integrated, the review it followed and the evidence | any act of a person |
  | basis changed | whoever observed it | the changed input, its previous identity and the items affected | that work reopened |
  | related conversation | the App | that one conversation was continued from, or forked from, another | a delegation |
  | external result | the manager or the person | an outside owner and the result files | dispatch or execution |

- **Facts kept apart.** For a work item: selected; brief prepared; dispatch observed; executing, as last observed; returned; reviewed; integrated. Each rests on its own record and none is inferred from another.
- **App observation.** An entry of a kind only the App records. The project's log is written by several hands, and anyone able to write the project can write a line that names the App. The App therefore writes each of its observations both to the project's log and to that project's subtree of the App's records store, and inside the App the store's copy and its order govern. A line in the project's log is an App observation only where the store holds the identical line; otherwise it is the claim of the recorder it names.
- **Native identity.** A native conversation is identified by Codex's own thread identity, qualified by the App-owned Codex home it lives in. An undertaking's name, a folder, a workflow choice or a description of an objective identifies no conversation and joins none.
- **Association.** The link from an observed native child to the brief it was dispatched for. It is recorded only from what the supplier makes observable about the dispatch. The delegating agent names the brief in the spawn prompt by one line giving the brief file's path in the fleet folder and the content identity of its bytes, and the App compares that with the file when it records the dispatch. The association is one of: a brief referenced, with that brief's content identity and the work item and undertaking that brief names, as read when the dispatch is recorded; a brief referenced whose content does not match the reference, with the same as read from the file then; a brief reference not resolved, with the reason (more than one brief named, a malformed reference, or a file that is absent, unreadable or not a brief); or no brief reference. A matching reference shows which brief the prompt named and what its bytes were when the dispatch was recorded, not that the child received or read it; a mismatch shows only that the file's bytes are not those referenced, not which of the two changed. The dispatch record carries the content identity of the prompt and never its text. Only a reference to a brief file that is read, matching or not, ties a child to a work item: the item and undertaking the brief named when the dispatch was recorded. That tie is part of the record and is never worked out again from the project's files; a brief file changed, replaced or superseded afterwards moves no worker, and the same brief identifier in another file ties nothing. A mismatch is shown with the tie. A child with any other association is still recorded as a child of its parent and is associated with no work item. Nothing else in a prompt, and nothing outside it, is read as a reference.
- **Delegation.** A dispatch is a native Codex child. A conversation continued under another role, or a fork, is a related conversation and never a dispatch. Work done outside Codex is an external result: an owner and result files, with no execution claimed.
- **Imported record.** A fleet record that entered the records store through an import package is quoted evidence of its source. It establishes no App observation and no native relationship of this installation.
- **Limit of a read.** Any file, line or copy the reader could not read, validate or reconcile, reported with its source and the reason.
- **Fleet folder.** `<project>/.chirality/fleet/`, holding `briefs/`, `graphs/<undertaking>/` and `coordination.fleet.jsonl`. No other location is offered in v4.0. The store's copy of the App's observations is `fleet/coordination.fleet.jsonl` in the project's subtree.

## Production and Verification Method — Praxeology

Records are JSON. A brief and a graph revision are each one file, created once and never overwritten; the log is one JSON object to a line and is only appended to. The content identity of a record file is the SHA-256 of its exact bytes, and two copies of an App observation are the same only when their bytes are identical. Agents and the person write briefs, graph revisions and their own log entries with ordinary file tools, so the files are usable with or without the App running; the App validates what it reads and writes only what it observes. Fixtures use invented material; a fixture shows how records behave, not that a supplier's child ran.

- **VER-001** — Executable check (observes the schema's verdict and the reader's output for a populated brief, a brief with an enforced limit lacking its mechanism, and a prepared brief with no dispatch).
- **VER-002** — Executable check (observes the reader's report of the current graph, its items and their needs for a two-revision fixture written by hand as files, and again after the selected file is altered).
- **VER-003** — Comparison with a named reference (Codex's native delegation events and thread reads for the same run): record an observed child, read it back and compare identities and parentage; plus an executable check of the no-dispatch, observation-ended, no-brief-reference, continued and forked cases.
- **VER-004** — Executable check (observes the facts read back before and after an interruption and a changed basis against what each of the fixture's records was written to establish).
- **VER-005** — Executable check (observes the state of a decision need, and the actor, recorder, content, scope and purpose read for it, against act records written outside this deliverable): a recorded act of the person with the recorder distinct, an agent's message alone, a tool's success alone, a return alone, a decide act choosing an alternative the package does not name, and unreadable act records.
- **VER-006** — Judgment by a named role (a reviewer who did not write the account): compare the account with the published schemas and with every reader and writer of the formats in the product, and follow the worked change through; plus an executable check of reading a record of an unknown version and of an unknown kind.
- **VER-007** — Executable check (observes what is read from hand-written files with no connector present, and compares each file's bytes before and after the read).
- **VER-008** — Judgment by a named role (an independent reviewer): trace each act of REQ-006 to its owner's code and read the fixture reports for any claim beyond record coverage.
- **VER-009** — Executable check (observes the files and the reader's output after an observation is recorded, after a forged line is added to the project's log, after the store's copy is damaged, shortened and rewritten, and after an import followed by a new observation).

## Governing Values and Decisions — Axiology

- **AX-001** — The files carry the coordination. Better means more of an undertaking's state can be recovered from the project's files alone, with fewer facts that must be fetched from a conversation or a running App; never by a record stating something that nobody recorded or observed.
- **AX-002** — A record says only what its recorder did or observed. Better means fewer work items left unknown because an observation Codex did supply was not recorded; never by filling an unobserved outcome, promoting one fact into another, or crediting a person with an act.
- **AX-003** — Limits are stated as they are. Better means a larger share of recorded limits name the mechanism that enforces them; never by recording a stated limit as enforced.
- **AX-004** — The formats stay stable for those who read them. Better means fewer format changes that force a consumer to change; never by letting a reader constrain what the writer may truthfully record, and never by rewriting an existing record.
- **TBD-001** — Whether fleet management goes beyond the accepted option is undecided (owner; PRD OQ-06). No scheduling, staffing or further management feature is part of these records.
- **TBD-002** — Whether briefs and work graphs stay keyed by an undertaking's name, as PRD V4-PM-02 words it, is undecided (owner). Until decided they are; no native conversation is identified or joined by that name.
- **TBD-003** — A native child is tied to its brief by the reference in its spawn prompt, as the Association term states; the supplier at the pin exposes that prompt on the spawn item, live and on history reads (decided by App Agent 0 under the owner's delegation). Where Codex supplies no prompt, the child is recorded with no brief reference and its prompt as not supplied, and nothing is inferred in its place: not from timing, order or a single prepared brief. No mark is set on a child's thread.
