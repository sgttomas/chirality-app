## Unavailable outcome table: precise consequences

- **Ordinary Stop:** retained stopping/cleanup-unavailable with no LT23 is truthful when completion proof is missing, but changes the existing completion/availability behavior. Do not label that mode full conformance. The design must expose actual attempts, known child exit and unresolved descendants separately.
- **Handshake failure:** preserving LT11 and blocking retry/replacement retains source facts, but can prevent the accepted bounded recovery route. That is an explicit unavailable capability, not evidence the automatic-restart obligation is satisfied.
- **EOF/LT12:** reaping withheld does not change why the source ended; no later cleanup may reattribute the earlier unexpected exit. Keeping a zombie/source indefinitely is not a selected final-reap policy. The post-LT12 row treatment remains held.
- **Reaped/ownership unknown:** no numeric action is supported without a new warrant. Repeated Stop is not an authority refresh. A persistent unresolved association may block the home; it must not be cleared merely to allow a fresh start.
- **LT20/current verification:** current-attempt cancellation remains process-action-free. Adding a block on later spawn for unresolved prior custody is a distinct availability constraint; the existing marker alone never proved prior cleanup.
- **Closing/grace expiry:** truthful cleanup-unavailable is supported; whether App exit proceeds or is prevented is not chosen by REC. “No blocking join” does not resolve who retains responsibility after the process exits. The current proposal correctly leaves that decision open.

U16's unexpected-exit show-survivors/person-action/overlap-wait treatment remains explicitly PROPOSED. The table's deny-replacement mode does not adopt that proposal, and must not silently supersede it as accepted permanent policy. Timing values remain implementation-owned/test values; timeout cannot settle group continuity or final reaping.
