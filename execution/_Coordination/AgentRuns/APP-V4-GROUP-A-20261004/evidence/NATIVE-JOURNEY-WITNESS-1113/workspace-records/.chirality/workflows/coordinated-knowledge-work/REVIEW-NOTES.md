## Defaults that should change when the evidence changes

**Keep the owner, but not at any cost.** Ownership preserves context and repair responsibility. Renew or transfer it when the actor cannot continue, competence is inadequate, or the scope has materially changed. Carry forward the work and its valid warrants, not an insistence on keeping a particular session alive.

**Reuse checks, but justify reliance.** A reviewer may inspect and rerun an established validator or calculation. Reproduction or independent derivation is warranted when a requirement calls for it, evidence is absent or contradictory, or a shared assumption is in question. The waste was repeating unchanged assurance without identifying what the repetition would establish.

**Batch integration, but preserve meaningful boundaries.** Combining stable contributions reduces repeated setup. A batch is too large when it obscures version changes, delays necessary feedback, or cannot be repaired safely. A working unit can stay small while its owner's responsibility and a later integration batch are larger.

**Keep the reviewer where context helps.** The reviewer who raised a finding normally confirms its repair. Change the reviewer when a specific competence, independence, or challenged-assumption problem warrants fresh perspective. A different agent following the same flawed premise is not automatically stronger assurance.

**Reassess support work instead of merely forbidding it.** The ledger segmentation was a useful maintenance improvement and reduced aggregate I/O. It did not explain all earlier delays. A bounded tool investment can be worthwhile, while a general redesign of tools or metadata may belong to another undertaking. Decide from the enabling outcome and cost, not from whether the work is labelled “infrastructure.”

**Preserve uncertainty without disguising incompleteness.** A faithful source corpus can retain an unreadable label as a graphic and an explicit uncertainty where the authorized outcome permits that. It cannot silently invent the label or call a missing source page uncertain merely to close the undertaking. A risk decision reserved for the human remains reserved.

## Read-through: from assignment to usable contribution

An engineering-document owner receives a whole-section responsibility with authoritative source versions, permitted changes, an independent reviewer, and a small ready-work limit. The owner first takes a representative section through source comparison, a saved table, review, and placement into the actual document. A continuation note is lost in placement; the team fixes the interface before multiplying that pattern across the corpus.

The owner then works through successive connected units without asking permission for each step. A frozen unit goes directly to its reviewer while the owner begins the next. The reviewer returns a missing negative qualifier to the same owner. That repair changes the requirement's meaning, so the reviewer directly compares it with the source. Existing table checks still apply because neither their content nor their dependencies changed.

The coordinator verifies that the returned version contains the repair and incorporates several compatible checked units. The actual placement check covers the affected links and continuations. It does not restart the unchanged cell review. Existing whole-section evidence establishes the remaining relationships, and the deliverable is returned with any genuine source uncertainty and later human acceptance explicit.

This is a walkthrough of the method, not a new audit or an observed prospective pilot.

## Read-through: a wrong shared decision and an interrupted write

This walkthrough adapts the reported shared-corpus and withdrawn-ruling incidents. The interrupted write is an illustrative extension, not a claim about the same historical sequence.

Three readers rely on a shared corpus. Independent review of that corpus probes inputs it leaves untested and exposes gaps despite the readers agreeing on existing cases. The coordinator resolves each interpretation within authority and pins it with additional shared cases every affected reader must pass. A contributor then challenges one ruling with evidence. The coordinator corrects the disposition within authority, identifies dependent work, and withdraws the old rule visibly. If that semantic choice is reserved, its decision owner resolves it.

The corpus owner prepares a successor while readers finish work whose basis remains valid. Each adopts the successor at a declared boundary. A return still contains the withdrawn rule; reading the relevant change reveals what a message acknowledgment and passing old tests could not. The owner repairs the behavior, and the reviewer checks the affected case. Other valid evidence is retained.

An integration subsequently executes but its acknowledgment is lost. The successor inspects the actual destination and finishes the pending check, rather than repeating the write. Unknown private job state is preserved and isolated from successor writes. The interruption does not require the team to recreate all completed evidence or restart the inquiry.

## Read-through: research rather than transcription

A team answers a decision question under a selected research workflow. Its early usable result is one argument with evidence, contrary findings, assumptions, and qualifications—not a pile of notes. Owners can challenge sources and derive conclusions because the assignment authorizes analysis; faithful-transcription constraints are not imported indiscriminately.

A source correction invalidates one argument but leaves another's independent warrant intact. The coordinator routes the affected repair, checks its consequences in the synthesis, and preserves the unaffected analysis. Any unmet required question remains visible. The team does not claim decision readiness solely from the count of completed research packets.

## What would validate the method further

Use the revised draft, after review and selection, on the next suitable authorized undertaking with multiple contributions and a real consumer. Choose a bounded deliverable whose result is useful; do not reopen the completed corpus solely to create evidence for this workflow. Carry an early example through the actual path and observe subsequent production, review, integration, and closure.

Use ordinary output and execution records to examine time to a usable result, aging ready work, repeated setup, late interface defects, repair effort, and supporting work relative to delivery. Distinguish observed intervals from estimates. If an interruption or changed input occurs, assess whether valid work and responsibility survive it; label simulations as simulations. Do not build a telemetry project to prove that avoiding excessive support work is beneficial.

Useful results would be earlier checked delivery, fewer avoidable waits and late discoveries, and recoverable changes without weakened acceptance conditions. Neither more pages nor fewer findings alone establishes improvement. Compare similar work, disclose complexity and staffing differences, and keep causal claims about models or controls within the evidence.
