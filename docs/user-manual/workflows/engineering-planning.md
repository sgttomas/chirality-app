# Prepare an engineering estimate and schedule

Use this method to prepare or revise an engineering estimate and a schedule
that account for the same work. It connects effort, cost, technical handoffs,
people's availability and the dates of required results.

Source: [Integrated engineering estimate and schedule](https://github.com/sgttomas/chirality/blob/main/workflows/integrated-engineering-estimate-schedule/WORKFLOW.md),
with its [detailed methods](https://github.com/sgttomas/chirality/blob/main/workflows/integrated-engineering-estimate-schedule/METHODS.md).
This is an instruction package, not a calculation program. App packaging and a
worked numerical example remain to be verified before this guide is issued.

## Bring

A sufficient work definition; required deliverables and handoffs; quantity and
effort assumptions; roles, calendars and availability; rates and their currency
and year; required dates; and the tools or files in which the result is needed.
Explain whether costs mean internal cost, charge-out or a complete fee.

Missing rates should leave costs unpriced, not zero. Unconfirmed resources must
remain assumptions. If the work definition is inadequate, clarify it before
presenting a total or finish date as a forecast.

## Example brief

> Prepare a provisional estimate and schedule for the defined design work.
> Use these quantities, effort assumptions, calendars and rates. Include
> checking and integration. Show missing inputs and unpriced work. Compare the
> effect of the vendor information arriving two weeks later. Do not change the
> accepted scope or commit people to dates.

Ask the agent to explain how it groups the work and which handoffs determine
when other work can proceed. Have it calculate the effort and costs using the
selected tools, then load the effort onto the schedule using actual availability.
Check that both views still describe the same work after each revision.

## Examine the result

Trace one significant deliverable from its inputs through production, checking
and delivery. Check quantities, units, rates, calendars and resource limits.
Confirm that shared work is counted once and that receiving or integration work
has not disappeared. Check external waits and the effect of incomplete inputs.

Compare scenarios on both cost and timing. Moving a date does not by itself
save effort. A lower effort estimate needs an explanation of what work or method
changed. Keep the assumptions and unresolved items with the result so another
person can assess it.

A calculated plan does not establish technical readiness, resource commitments
or an accepted execution baseline. Those require the responsible people's
review and decisions.
